**Spot-check:** the FY2024 figure of 13,387 players and 408 teams was read directly from the SSF PDF and matches the Flag Football Global article. The FY2020 and FY2022 figures come only from Flag Football Global's summary of SSF data **[?]**.

**Trend:** a peak of about 20,700 players in 1992, a fall to about 15,600 by 2000, a partial recovery to about 19,500 around 2010, and a steady decline to about 13,400 by FY2024 (about −15% in four years).

## 4. Where the sport lives: universities

Japanese football is mainly a **university** sport; most players first play at 18.

| Program | Fact | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Kwansei Gakuin Fighters (Hyogo) | Record 34 Koshien Bowl titles (4 shared) plus 1 Rice Bowl; 6 straight Koshien titles to 2023 | [JA] ja.wikipedia 関西学院大学ファイターズ |
| Nihon University Phoenix (Tokyo) | Historic rival of Kwansei from 1955; disbanded 2023 after drugs case; volunteer team back in the top tier's lower group for 2026 | [JA] ja.wikipedia 日本大学フェニックス |
| Rikkyo, Meiji, Waseda, Keio | Founding schools of 1934 | [JA] JAFA 1934 page |
| Kanto and Kansai student federations | Run the college leagues; the Koshien Bowl (December, at Koshien Stadium) is the national college final | [JA] ja.wikipedia 関東/関西学生アメリカンフットボール連盟 |

JAFA now runs "crossover" combines with NFL drills to recruit high-schoolers from other sports into university football, because many students quit sport when they enter university (JAFA FY2025 report).
