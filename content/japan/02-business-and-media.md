## 1. The newspaper-sponsor model (1947 onward)

Japanese football was built on newspaper sponsorship, the same model as high-school baseball.

| Event | Sponsor | What it shows | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Koshien Bowl (from 1947) | Mainichi Shimbun | Mainichi ran the first college final and covered the losses (about 700 fans). | [JA] JAFA year page 1947 |
| Rice Bowl (from 1948) | Nikkan Sports | Asahi and Yomiuri both declined first. | [JA] JAFA year page 1948 |
| First NFL game, 1976 | Mainichi Shimbun, Sports Nippon | Called the "Mainichi Star Bowl". | [JA] ja.wikipedia アメリカンボウル; [EN] en.wikipedia American Bowl |
| American Bowl, Tokyo Dome | Toshiba (to 1991), then Budweiser | Big consumer brands as title sponsors in the bubble years. | [JA] ja.wikipedia アメリカンボウル |
| Mirage Bowl → Coca-Cola Bowl (1977–93) | Mirage, then Coca-Cola | US college games in Tokyo for 17 years. | [JA] ja.wikipedia ミラージュボウル; [EN] en.wikipedia Coca-Cola Classic |
| Ivy Bowl (1989–96) | Epson | Ivy League all-stars vs Japan all-stars; the Ivy side won all 7 games JAFA lists. | [JA] JAFA year pages 1989–96; [EN] Ivy League release |

## 3. The NFL's own presence in Japan

| Item | Detail | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Exhibitions | 14 NFL exhibition games in Japan from 1976 to 2005; Japan was the league's earliest overseas stage. | [JA] Jiji 2026-10-30 |
| Why it stopped | Once the NFL moved to regular-season games abroad, it chose the UK, Mexico and Germany. Japan's time difference makes it hard to match a US TV window with a local crowd. | [JA] Jiji 2026-10-30 |
| What changed | Melbourne, September 2026: 100,000+ fans for a Friday 10:35 a.m. local kickoff (Thursday night in the US), which shows an Asia-Pacific slot can work. | [JA] Jiji 2026-10-30 |
| Signals | Goodell (late Oct 2026): Tokyo "next year or the year after". Brett Gosper, NFL head of Europe and Asia-Pacific, rates Japan's potential highly and cites Ohtani-era interest in US sport. | [JA] Jiji 2026-10-30 |
| Club rights | The Rams hold Japan marketing rights under the Global Markets Program (set up in 2022; Rams added Japan in 2024). | [JA] Jiji 2026-10-30 |
| Talent pipeline | IPP (since 2017): 70 international players have signed with NFL teams; 22 were on rosters in Dec 2025. Kansei Matsuzawa (Tokyo-born kicker, Hawaii) is in the 2026 class; Raiders practice squad as of Aug 31, 2026. | [JA] NFL Japan 2025-12-15; [EN] en.wikipedia Kansei Matsuzawa |
| Grassroots | NFL Academy combines planned in Tokyo and Osaka, December 2026. JAFA ran "crossover athlete" combines with NFL drills for non-football high-schoolers in 2025–26 (12–40 people each). | [JA] NFL Japan news; JAFA FY2025 report |

## 4. Top-league business: the X League

The X League (Japan's top corporate/club league) rebuilt itself in 2026 as "X Premier".

| Item | Figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Licences | 11 of 13 applicants licensed for 2026; Fukuoka SUNS refused | [JA] Nikkan Sports 2025-08-04; Nikkei |
| Licence floor | At least ¥200M a year in business spending per club | [JA] Nikkei (licence article) |
| Licence pillars | Attendance targets, minimum pro players and coaches, mandatory kids/junior teams, hometown games | [JA] Nikkan Sports 2025-08-04 |
| Ownership | 2 company teams (Panasonic, Fujitsu) and 9 clubs | [JA] Gendai |
| League budget | Each club pays a ¥23M common fee; 11 × ¥23M is roughly the league's whole budget. 11 staff. No title partner. Volunteer board of about 15. | [JA] Gendai (Nishimura interview) |
| Previous-season attendance | 1,639 a game on average | [JA] Nikkei (2026, paywalled; figure in the visible summary) |
| Opening day 2026 | Tokyo Dome, free tickets; reported at 14,374 (24,299 across two opening games) | **[?]** from a search snippet; page not re-verified |
| Rice Bowl, Jan 2026 | 20,214 (17,885 the year before) | [JA] JAFA FY2025 report |
| Expansion targets named | Kyushu, Sendai, Hiroshima, Nagoya | [JA] Nikkan Sports / Nikkei |

The new league chair, Daisuke Nishimura (ex-B.League club executive), told Gendai the league is "endangered" (絶滅危惧リーグ) and that football could vanish from Japan within 5–10 years without change. He also said full professional status is not realistic. See file 04 for the 2026 governance crisis.
