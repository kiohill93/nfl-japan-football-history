# 05 — Health and safety

Research date: 2026-11-05. **[JA]** Japanese source, **[EN]** English source, **[?]** unconfirmed.

Schools and parents decide whether children play, and the sport's serious accidents get national coverage.

## 1. Serious accidents in numbers

| Period | Figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1981–1989 | 22 deaths nationwide (JAFA data) | [JA] Yomiuri 2024-10-01 |
| 1991–2003 (Kanto) | 235 concussions and 20 serious head injuries, of which 18 acute subdural hematomas and 3 deaths | [JA] Fukuda (2020), J. of the Japanese Society of Athletic Training, citing Fujitani et al. |
| 1991–2010 (Kanto games) | Head and neck injuries were 16.8% of game injuries | same |
| Since 2006 | 38 serious head injuries (bleeding in the skull) reported to JAFA, 4 of them fatal | [JA] JAFA alert 2026-08-25 |
| 2026 (to August) | 9 serious accidents, the most since 2006: 4 acute subdural hematomas (1 death), 1 brain contusion, 2 internal-organ injuries, 2 cervical fractures | [JA] JAFA alert 2026-08-25 |
| 2026-07-11 / 08-08 | A Chiba University player injured in July died in August | **[?]** search snippet only; page not opened |

JAFA's analysis of the 38 cases: they cluster in **August** (heat), among **first- and second-year players**, and on **small teams**, and have risen **since the COVID years (2020–22)**.

## 2. How Japan compares with the US

The 2020 review by Takashi Fukuda (University of Tsukuba) summarizes Japanese research:

| Measure | Japan | US | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fatality rate from serious head injury, adjusted for participation | about 20 times the US rate | baseline | Fukuda 2020, citing Kawahara |
| Concussion rate in games (college) | 11.1% (3.9 per 1,000 athlete-exposures) | 6.8% (2.34 per 1,000), NCAA | Fukuda 2020 |
| Concussion rate in practice (college) | 8.4% (0.6 per 1,000) | 5.5% (0.21 per 1,000) | Fukuda 2020 |
| Head impacts per practice (college) | 14.3, about the same as in games (18.1) | about 7 in practice, about 15 in games | Fukuda 2020 |
| Field-practice frequency through the year | about twice the US | baseline | Fukuda 2020 |

**Why Japan is worse, per the review:** most Japanese players start the sport at university, so coaches run many contact drills and full scrimmages to teach beginners quickly. The review notes that "heads-up" tackling (shoulder-first, head out of the hit) has spread in Japan and lowers concussion risk without hurting tackling.

## 3. Heat

| Date | Event / rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 2013-08-08 | Heatstroke death of a player at a high school (Minoh Jiyu Gakuen, Osaka) | [JA] ja.wikipedia 箕面自由学園; JAFA notices |
| 2013-08-29 | JAFA emergency panel's rules: **no game if WBGT (heat index) is above 31°C or air temperature above 35°C**; extra precautions above WBGT 27°C or 30°C; recheck at half-time and delay the second half if needed | [JA] JAFA heatstroke prevention proposal, 2013-08-29 |
| From about 2005 | Doctors at Kansai league games; when the pre-game heat index is over the limit, each quarter is cut by 1 minute (example from a Sept 2024 game) | [JA] Yomiuri 2024-10-01 |
| 2026-08-25 | JAFA asks teams to use sports drinks plus salt, check emergency hospitals near summer-camp sites | [JA] JAFA alert 2026-08-25 |

## 4. Concussion rules

| Date | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 2013-09-07 | JAFA notice: a player judged to have a concussion may not return to play that day; any return must be agreed with a doctor and be gradual. JAFA notes rugby, judo and soccer already had guidelines. | [JA] JAFA safety committee notice 2013-09-07 |
| Current | Graduated return-to-play steps (step 1: everyday activity, at least 24 hours symptom-free; step 2a: light aerobic exercise; and so on) | [JA] JAFA return-to-play table |
| Current | JAFA player-safety handbook and a 10-point head-injury guide (Japanese Society of Clinical Sports Medicine, revised 2016) | [JA] JAFA safety page |
| 2026-08-25 | Stop play after a strong blow to the head and watch for symptoms; call an ambulance for loss of consciousness, seizures, vomiting or severe headache; JAFA recommends a head MRI for new players to screen for conditions that bleed easily | [JA] JAFA alert 2026-08-25 |
| Ongoing | JAFA makes leagues report every serious injury and heatstroke case each July before players can register; a national safety committee reviews the reports. Annual medical-science seminars since at least 2015. | [JA] Fukuda 2020; JAFA seminar programs |
