# 06 — Military and US ties

Research date: 2026-11-05. **[JA]** Japanese source, **[EN]** English source, **[?]** unconfirmed.

## 1. Missionaries and Nisei (1934–1941)

- The sport was brought in by **Paul Rusch**, an American lay Episcopal missionary teaching at Rikkyo University. He founded the Tokyo student federation in October 1934 and chaired it. [JA] JAFA 1934 page; ja.wikipedia ポール・ラッシュ
- About **80% of early players were Japanese-American Nisei** from Hawaii studying in Tokyo. [JA] ja.wikipedia 立教大学ラッシャーズ
- The first official game (Thanksgiving 1934) was a Yokohama expatriate club against Tokyo students, watched by Prince Chichibu and US Ambassador Joseph Grew. [JA] JAFA 1934 page; [EN] FISU
- During the war the sport was renamed 鎧球 ("armor ball") and then stopped (1943–44). [JA] JAFA year pages

## 3. US service bowl games in Japan

These were games between US military teams, played in Japan. One fan-run site (luckyshow.org) lists them; crowd figures are **[?]** single-source.

| Game | Year | Result | Crowd | Venue |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Atom Bowl | 1946-01-01 | Isahaya Tigers 14, Nagasaki Bears (2nd Marine Div.) 13 | about 2,000 | Nagasaki, on a field cleared from bomb rubble |
| Rice Bowl 1 | 1946-01-01 | 11th Airborne 25, 41st Division 12 | 15,000 | Tokyo |
| Rice Bowl 4 | 1949-01-01 | Army Ground Forces 13, Air Force 7 | 20,000 | Tokyo |
| Rice Bowl 9 | 1955-12-31 | Air Force 33, Army 14 | 40,000 | Tokyo |
| Rice Bowl 11 | 1958-12-20 | Air Force All-Stars 20, Army All-Stars 0 | 78,000 | National Stadium, Tokyo |
| Torii Bowls | 1954–59 | Service all-star games | — | Meiji (Jingu) Stadium, Tokyo |
| Silk Bowl | 1967-12-24 | Kanto student all-stars vs US Navy Atsugi Flyers | — | — |
| Ocean Bowl 1 | 1995-02-05 | Yokota Raiders (US Yokota Air Base) 18, Osaka Ringostars 9 | — | — |
| USAF Japan league finals | 2003–07 | Yokota, Camp Foster (Okinawa), Yokosuka teams | — | US bases |

The Atom Bowl is confirmed by a second source: Paleofuture (2018) cites a January 1946 wire story and a 2005 *New York Times* interview, and says Heisman winner Angelo Bertelli and ex-Chicago Bear Bill Osmanski played. [EN] Paleofuture

**What it shows:** for about 15 years after the war, the biggest football crowds in Japan were for US service games (up to 78,000 in 1958), while Japanese college finals drew hundreds to a few thousand. JAFA notes "active exchange" games with US forces' teams in 1960 [JA] JAFA year page 1960. Base teams (Yokota, Yokosuka, Okinawa) still play, but no recent games against Japanese teams were found.
