# 01 — American football in Japan: timeline, 1934 to November 2026

Research date: 2026-11-05. Every row says what it rests on. "JAFA year page" means the Japan American Football Association's 90-year history series, one page per year, at `https://americanfootball.jp/top_news/<234 + (year − 1934)>/` (for example 1934 = `/top_news/234/`, 1992 = `/top_news/292/`). Those pages were opened for every year from 1934 to 2023.

Key: **[JA]** Japanese source, **[EN]** English source, **[?]** not confirmed by a second source, **[conflict]** sources disagree (explained under the table).

## 1. Founding and prewar (1934–1945)

| Date | Event | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1934-10-28 | Tokyo student federation (東京学生米式蹴球連盟) founded at Rikkyo University's Building 5. Paul Rusch, a lay Episcopal missionary teaching at Rikkyo, chairs it. 3 schools (Rikkyo, Meiji, Waseda) and 66 registered players. | [JA] JAFA year page 1934 (`/top_news/234/`), JAFA prewar page (`/top_news/232/`) |
| 1934-11-29 | First official game, Thanksgiving Day, at Meiji Jingu Gaien Stadium: Yokohama Country & Athletic Club vs an all-Tokyo student team. Prince Chichibu and US Ambassador Joseph Grew attend. | [JA] JAFA 1934 page; [EN] JAFA English page; FISU history |
| 1934-12-08 | First inter-college game, Rikkyo vs Meiji. About 80% of early players were Japanese-American Nisei from Hawaii. | [JA] ja.wikipedia 立教大学ラッシャーズ; JAFA 1934 page |
| 1938 | JAFA (日本アメリカンフットボール協会) founded. First East–West student all-star game draws about 25,000 and is broadcast on radio. | [JA] JAFA year page 1938 |
| 1940 | Wartime renaming: the sport becomes 鎧球 ("armor ball") to avoid an English name. | [JA] JAFA year page 1940; ja.wikipedia アメリカンフットボール |
| 1943–44 | Play suspended as students are mobilized. | [JA] JAFA year pages 1943–44 |

**[conflict] First-game crowd.** JAFA's English page says 15,000. JAFA's own 50-year history (reprinted on its Japanese site) and the FISU article say about 20,000. This site uses "15,000–20,000".

## 2. Occupation and the bowl era (1945–1960)

| Date | Event | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1945-09-20 | Rusch returns to Japan as a US Army lieutenant colonel in the occupation's Civil Information and Education section. | [JA] ja.wikipedia ポール・ラッシュ; [EN] en.wikipedia Paul Rusch |
| 1946-01-01 | Occupation service teams play bowl games in Japan: "Rice Bowl" in Tokyo (11th Airborne vs 41st Division, about 15,000) and the "Atom Bowl" in Nagasaki (about 2,000 servicemen, Heisman winner Angelo Bertelli playing). See file 06. | [EN] luckyshow.org service bowls list; Paleofuture |
| 1946-06-29 | Kanto student federation re-formed, using surplus US Army equipment. | [JA] JAFA postwar page (`/top_news/32/`) |
| 1947-04-13 | First Koshien Bowl (college final), run by the Mainichi Shimbun. About 700 spectators; band of the US 25th Division plays. Keio beats Doshisha. Mainichi covers the losses. | [JA] JAFA year page 1947; ja.wikipedia 甲子園ボウル |
| 1948-01-17 | First Japanese Rice Bowl (East–West student all-stars), sponsored by Nikkan Sports after Asahi and Yomiuri decline. Played at "Nile Kinnick Stadium", the occupation name for Jingu Gaien. The name borrows from the occupation forces' own Far East "Rice Bowl". Rusch makes the ceremonial kickoff. JAFA re-forms afterwards. | [JA] JAFA year page 1948; ja.wikipedia ライスボウル |
| 1955 | Nihon University vs Kwansei Gakuin rivalry begins; it dominates the Koshien Bowl for decades. | [JA] JAFA year page 1955; ja.wikipedia 関西学院大学ファイターズ |
| 1958-12-20 | US service all-star "Rice Bowl" at the National Stadium, Tokyo, listed at 78,000. | [EN] luckyshow.org **[?]** single source |
| 1960 | JAFA notes active exchange games with US forces' teams. | [JA] JAFA year page 1960 |

## 3. Television, companies and growth (1961–1992)

| Date | Event | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1961 | First league game on TV. Mitsubishi Jushi forms the first company (corporate) team. | [JA] JAFA year page 1961 |
| 1970 | Magazine TOUCHDOWN launches (published until October 2016). | [JA] JAFA year page 1970 |
| 1971 | Utah State tours: JAFA calls it the first year of internationalization. Japan corporate league founded. | [JA] JAFA year page 1971 |
| 1975 | Member schools grow fast. | [JA] JAFA year page 1975 |
| 1976-08-16 | First NFL game in Japan: Cardinals 20, Chargers 10 at Korakuen Stadium ("Mainichi Star Bowl", sponsored by Mainichi Shimbun and Sports Nippon). | [JA] ja.wikipedia アメリカンボウル; [EN] en.wikipedia American Bowl |
| 1977–1993 | Mirage Bowl / Coca-Cola Classic: US college regular-season games in Tokyo. | [JA] ja.wikipedia ミラージュボウル; [EN] en.wikipedia Coca-Cola Classic |
| 1978 | 112 member universities. After a serious injury, JAFA warns that growth is outpacing safety. | [JA] JAFA year page 1978 |
| 1979 | Notre Dame visits. Paul Rusch dies. | [JA] JAFA year page 1979 |
| 1983 | Rice Bowl becomes the national final: college champion vs corporate champion. | [JA] JAFA year page 1983; ja.wikipedia ライスボウル |
| 1985 | Corporate football association founded. Matsushita Denko (today's Panasonic) is in the first top division; it is the only original top-tier team still there. | [JA] JAFA year page 1985; news lead, Oct 2026 |
| 1987 | Record total attendance for the season. | [JA] JAFA year page 1987 |
| 1988 | Tokyo Super Bowl (corporate final) moves to the new Tokyo Dome. Ivy Bowl begins (Jan 1989 – 1996). | [JA] JAFA year page 1988; [EN] Ivy League release |
| 1989-08-06 | American Bowl at Tokyo Dome: Rams 16, 49ers 13 (OT). Toshiba sponsors American Bowls to 1991, then Budweiser. | [JA] ja.wikipedia アメリカンボウル; [EN] Washington Post archive headline (search result) |
| 1990 | Corporate company teams and club teams merged into one league. | [JA] JAFA year page 1990 |
| 1992 | **Peak registration**: 424 teams, 20,683 players (see file 07). NFL on NHK-BS and Nippon TV each week. | [JA] JAFA year page 1992 |
| 1993 | First women's game (later the Queen Bowl). | [JA] JAFA year page 1993 |

## 5. Crisis and reform (2018–2026)
