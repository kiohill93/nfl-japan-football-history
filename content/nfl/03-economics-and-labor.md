Revenue roughly tripled from 2010 to 2025. Per-team national money went from $99.9M (2010) to more than $450M (2025). Sources: Wikipedia NFL; [Sportico, July 2026](https://www.sportico.com/leagues/football/2026/nfl-2025-national-revenue-per-team-1234939659/).

Revenue mix, 2025 (Sportico): TV about $12B a year; tickets and suites $4.35B (19% of team revenue); national distribution about 62% of an average team's revenue.

- The cap grew 8.7× from 1994 to 2026.
- In the 2010 uncapped year, teams that front-loaded deals were later penalised: Cowboys $10M, Redskins $36M ([Wikipedia: Salary cap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salary_cap)).
- 2020 CBA: the COVID revenue loss was spread over four seasons, with a $175M cap floor for 2021 ([Wikipedia: 1982 NFL strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_NFL_strike), which covers later labor history).

## 4. Free agency

| Year | Milestone | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1957 | *Radovich*: football is covered by antitrust law, so player restraints can be challenged | [Wikipedia: Radovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radovich_v._National_Football_League) |
| 1963–76 | Rozelle Rule: the commissioner set compensation for any team that signed a free agent, which in practice ended free agency | Wikipedia: Rozelle Rule |
| 1989–92 | Plan B: teams protect 37 players and the rest are free to move | Wikipedia: Free agent |
| 1992 | *McNeil* verdict against Plan B | Wikipedia: Freeman McNeil |
| 1993 | Reggie White signs with Green Bay for 4 years/$17M, the defining first free-agent deal (his Eagles deal had been 4 years/$6M) | [Wikipedia: Reggie White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reggie_White) |
| 1993– | Unrestricted free agency after 4 seasons, paired with the cap and the franchise tag | Wikipedia NFLPA |

## 5. Antitrust history

| Year | Case or law | Outcome | Why it matters | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1957 | *Radovich v. NFL* | Football not exempt from antitrust law (unlike baseball) | Every league practice can be challenged in court | Wikipedia: Radovich |
| 1961 | Sports Broadcasting Act | Lets leagues pool TV rights (the AFL–ABC deal had shown the model) | Basis of equal TV revenue sharing | [Wikipedia: SBA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_Broadcasting_Act_of_1961) |
| 1966 | PL 89-800 | Merger exempt from antitrust law. Rozelle promised Congress teams would not move | Merger possible | [Wikipedia: franchise moves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Football_League_franchise_moves_and_mergers) |
| 1982 | *Raiders v. NFL* (Davis) | Raiders allowed to move to LA **[unconfirmed details]** | Limits on blocking moves | Wikipedia: franchise moves |
| 1986 | *USFL v. NFL* | Jury found the NFL an illegal monopoly but awarded $1, trebled to $3. The USFL got a cheque for $3.76 in 1990 (76¢ interest). USFL folded | The NFL's last serious rival league | [Wikipedia: USFL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Football_League) |
| 1976 / 1992 | *Mackey*, *McNeil* | Player restraints struck down | Led to free agency | above |
| 2010 | *American Needle v. NFL* | Unanimous: the 32 teams are separate actors and can conspire under Sherman Act §1 (case was about an exclusive Reebok headwear licence) | No "single entity" shield | [Wikipedia: American Needle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Needle,_Inc._v._National_Football_League) |
| 2024– | Sunday Ticket class action | $4.6B jury award (over $14B trebled) set aside by the judge; on appeal | Tests how far the SBA reaches | House SBA report |
