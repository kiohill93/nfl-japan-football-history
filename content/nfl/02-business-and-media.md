# 02 — Business and media

Compiled 2026-11-05. **[unconfirmed]** = search snippet only or a single weak source.

## 1. How the league is organised

- **Structure:** 32 privately owned clubs (the Green Bay Packers are fan-owned; a share cost $300 in the 2026 report) plus a league office that negotiates national media, sponsorship and licensing. Owners vote on rules, relocation and new owners. Sources: [Yahoo Sports, Packers 2026](https://sports.yahoo.com/articles/y-sports-biz-cheese-squeezed-financially-154014025.html), [Wikipedia: NFL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Football_League).
- **Long-term ownership:** 23 of 32 teams have had the same owner or family for 20 years or more. Private-equity funds may hold minority stakes since 2024. Source: [Quartz, Forbes 2026 valuations](https://qz.com/nfl-most-valuable-team-2026).
- **Tax status:** the league office was a 501(c)(6) non-profit trade association until it gave up the status in 2015. Goodell called it a "distraction", and the change cost about $10M a year in tax. The clubs were always taxable. Source: Wikipedia NFL.
- **Antitrust footing:** the 1961 Sports Broadcasting Act lets the clubs sell TV rights as one package. *American Needle* (2010) held that clubs are separate businesses for antitrust purposes. See 03, Economics and labor.

Notes:
- Owners can opt out of the current deals after the 2029 season (Sportico; Quartz says after 2029/2030). The next round of talks is the biggest money event on the horizon.

- Legal pressure on the SBA: in June 2024 a jury awarded $4.6B (trebled to over $14B) in the Sunday Ticket class action. The judge set the verdict aside and it is on appeal. A House Judiciary staff report (June 2026) calls the SBA a "special-interest antitrust exemption". Source: House SBA report.

## 7. Stadium financing

- Most NFL stadiums since the 1990s used a mix of public money (local taxes, bonds) and private money. The league also lends to clubs under its G-4 (later G-5) stadium loan programme. **[unconfirmed: not opened in this research; check before using numbers]**
- Relocations follow stadium deals: Rams to LA (2016, privately financed SoFi Stadium), Chargers to LA (2017), Raiders to Las Vegas (2020, Allegiant Stadium with public hotel-tax funding). Source for the moves: [Wikipedia: franchise moves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Football_League_franchise_moves_and_mergers). The financing details are **[unconfirmed]**.
- Gap: no public-subsidy totals were collected.

### 8c. Global Markets Program (GMP)

- Launched in 2022. Gives clubs marketing rights in foreign markets. All 32 clubs and 22 markets take part in 2026, and Italy was added that year. Source: [NFL GMP release, 2026](https://media.nfl.com/news-and-releases/international/nfl-s-global-markets-program-expands-in-2026-with-addition-of-it).
- **Japan is assigned to one club only, the Los Angeles Rams.** The Rams also hold Australia, China, South Korea, Mexico, New Zealand and the UAE. Big European markets such as Germany and the UK each have many clubs. Same source.
- The league's head of international is Gerrit Meier. League offices abroad include Mexico (opened 1998) and Germany (Düsseldorf, 2023) ([Wikipedia: History of the NFL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_National_Football_League)).
