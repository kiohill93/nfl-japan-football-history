03 — Economics and salaries

Research date: 2026-11-05. [JA] Japanese source, [EN] English source, [?] unconfirmed, [conflict] sources disagree.

1. The basic model: company employees who play

Japanese top-level football has always run on the corporate sports model: players are company employees (or hold other jobs) and play after work. The 2026 licence announcement said 99% of X League players have another job (Nikkan Sports, 2025-08-04). A 1989 survey of college all-star players found 54.7% were joining companies with a corporate-league team and 14.7% companies with associate or planned teams (JAFA year page 1989, citing American Football Magazine, March 1990). In other words, the sport was a hiring channel for big employers in the bubble years.

06 — Military and US ties

Research date: 2026-11-05. [JA] Japanese source, [EN] English source, [?] unconfirmed.

1. Missionaries and Nisei (1934–1941)

  • The sport was brought in by Paul Rusch, an American lay Episcopal missionary teaching at Rikkyo University. He founded the Tokyo student federation in October 1934 and chaired it. [JA] JAFA 1934 page; ja.wikipedia ポール・ラッシュ

08 — Viewership and fans: how many people watch football in Japan, and how that changed

Research date: 2026-11-05. [JA] Japanese source, [EN] English source, [?] unconfirmed, [conflict] sources disagree.

Summary

  • Crowds: football's biggest crowds in Japan came between 1988 and 1997, when the Rice Bowl, the corporate final and the American Bowls each drew 40,000–56,000 at Tokyo Dome. Today the Rice Bowl draws about 20,000, the Koshien Bowl about 24,000 and a regular X League game about 1,000–1,600.
  • TV ratings: almost none have been published. The only football ratings found are from the 1970s: 11.4% for the first Japan Bowl (Nippon TV, 1976) and 7.4% for a recorded Kansai college game (MBS, 1977). Neither NHK nor DAZN publishes NFL audiences for Japan.
  • Surveys: football barely registers. In CRS's 2025 national survey it ranks 25th among "sports I'm interested in" (3.1%). It isn't on CRS's list of pro sports at all, and it is missing from the top lists in the SSF and MURC × Macromill surveys.
  • Online interest: Japanese Wikipedia views of the American football article have kept pace with baseball's (about 70% of the baseball article's views in both 2016 and 2025), but the absolute numbers are small. The spikes come from scandal (2018) and a Japanese player (2026).
