Compiled November 2026 from public sources.

Data honesty: these are research notes compiled from public sources, not official NFL or JAFA data. Numbers are copied as the sources give them. Values that are unconfirmed, conflicting or seen only in a search snippet stay in the text and in the CSV note columns, and are left out of the charts. Missing years are missing, not zero. How the research was done.

Compiled 2026-11-05 from the NFL and Japan research on this site. One doc for learning the major names behind both stories.

How to read it - Part 1 covers the NFL's own story: founders, commissioners, owners, union leaders, media, players, coaches, health and safety, and the international side. "File" numbers in Part 1 point to the numbered sections of the NFL history page. - Part 2 covers football in Japan: founders, federation and league leaders, coaches, players, media and culture, business, and NFL people working on Japan today. "File" numbers in Part 2 point to the numbered sections of the Japan page. - Each part starts with "If you only learn these 15". - Name order: Part 1 writes Japanese names given name first (Takayuki Hioki); Part 2 uses family name first, as in Japan (Hioki Takayuki), with kanji. - Markers: [unconfirmed], [?] = not confirmed in an opened source; [conflict] = sources disagree; [JA]/[EN] = Japanese or English source.

People in both stories: Roger Goodell, Brett Gosper, Frank Takahashi (promoter of the 1976 Tokyo game), Takayuki Hioki (Sports Branding Japan, the NFL's Japan liaison office), and the Rams (Stan Kroenke; Stephanie Cheng, head of Rams international).

1. Founders and pioneers (1934–1950s)

# Name Years Role What they did Source
1 Paul Rusch ポール・ラッシュ 1897–1979 Founder Came to Japan in 1925 to help rebuild the YMCA after the 1923 earthquake and taught at Rikkyo. In 1934 he organized football for Nisei students and set up the Tokyo student league with Rikkyo, Waseda and Meiji as its first chairman. Returned in 1945 as an occupation officer and helped restart the sport. Named "father of Japanese football" in 1961. The Rice Bowl MVP award is the Paul Rusch Trophy. [JA] JAFA HoF
2 Matsumoto Takizo 松本瀧蔵 1901–1958 Co-founder Harvard-educated Meiji professor; secretary-general of the 1934 league and referee of Japan's first game. Succeeded Rusch as league chairman in 1942; later a Diet member and deputy foreign minister. [JA] JAFA HoF
3 Asano Ryozo 浅野良三 1889–1965 First president Son of the founder of the Asano zaibatsu, Harvard-educated head of Nippon Kokan (NKK). First president of the student league (1937) and of the national association (1938); linked football with the business world. [JA] JAFA HoF
4 Ogawa Tokuji 小川徳治 1905–2001 Organizer Rikkyo professor who ran the 1934 league's money, fields and equipment, and pushed to make gear in Japan. Twice chaired the national association in the 1960s. [JA] JAFA HoF
5 Hanaoka Atsushi 花岡惇 1914–1994 Nisei pioneer Hawaii-born Nisei who founded Meiji's team and played in the 1934 first game. Coached Meiji for 16 years and the East all-stars for over a decade, spreading US tactics. [JA] JAFA HoF
6 Inoue Motoyuki 井上素行 1915–1987 Nisei pioneer Hawaii-born; helped found Waseda's team, played in the 1934 game and the 1936 US tour, and later wrote the first official rulebook. [JA] JAFA HoF
7 Hoshina Susumu 保科進 1906–2000 Nisei pioneer, coach Came from Hawaii in 1935, founded Hosei's team and coached college football for 57 years, still on the field at 91. [JA] JAFA HoF
8 Matsuba Tokusaburo 松葉徳三郎 1903–1981 Founder of Kansai football Osaka YMCA sports director who saw football at the 1932 Los Angeles Olympics and started Kansai University's team (1935), the first in western Japan. [JA] JAFA HoF
9 Hattori Shingo 服部慎吾 1913–1995 Postwar rebuilder Rikkyo center, then a soldier. Rebuilt the national association in 1948 and chaired it for 5 years; kept the records of the pre-war and postwar game. [JA] JAFA HoF
10 Misumi Shuichi 三隅珠一 1924–1975 Father of high-school football PE teacher at Ikeda Middle School (Osaka) who held Japan's first official touch-football game (1945), started the national high-school touch tournament (1954) and the tackle championship (1970). The best back at the high-school final wins the Misumi Cup. [JA] JAFA HoF
